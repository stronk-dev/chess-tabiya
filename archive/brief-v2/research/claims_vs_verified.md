# Claims versus verified behavior

## Evidence levels

- **Official capability:** documented by the product/project owner.
- **Hands-on field report:** supplied by the user or a forum participant.
- **Independent research:** academic or third-party measurement.
- **Inference:** conclusion drawn from documented capabilities.

## Key distinctions

### Chess Endgame Training

Officially supports outcome play, draws, move-list branching and manual what-if exploration. The user's speed/UX criticism is a hands-on report and should be reproduced during Phase 0.

### Noctie

Officially supports selected openings, human-statistical opening play, full-game continuation, arbitrary positions and endgames. The absence of a persistent branch-comparison curriculum is an inference from public materials and should be verified hands-on.

### ChessDojo

Officially prescribes fixed-position opening/middlegame sparring and repeated play from both colors. Its effectiveness relative to software is not experimentally established here.

### Maia-3

Officially provides rating-conditioned human move prediction. The need for a plan-coherence layer is an engineering inference based on the difference between next-move prediction and long-horizon policy.

### Paid SaaS

The claim is not that paid SaaS “does not exist.” It is that paid access is not a kill criterion for a self-hosted build, and that the complete workflow is not clearly available as one integrated local tool.

## User-observed, pending independent benchmark

- Chess Endgame Training has the right full-outcome interaction concept but feels slow and cumbersome in practice. The feature set is verified from the product documentation; the performance and UX judgment must be reproduced in Phase 0.
