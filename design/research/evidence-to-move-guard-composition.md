with the already-measured, disclosed 250-cp `ErrorGuard`?

**Method:** preregistered disposable development screen. The committed-before-run plan is

## 1. Verdict

**The declared guard repairs the severe tail but does not make this evidence selector stable enough
to earn its 1.0 research gate.** The two held-out folds fail different predeclared clauses. `[V]`

- Validation admits **113/117 human moves (96.6%)**, and every rating band exceeds the 85% floor,
  but guarded combined cross entropy is **2.312672**, slightly worse than guarded engine's
  **2.293956**. `[V]`
- Once-read confirmation admits **101/108 (93.5%)** and guarded combined is substantially better
  (**1.862308 vs 2.249685**), but the 1000–1399 band admits only **14/17 (82.4%)**, below the fixed
  85% anti-pooling floor. `[V]`

The preregistration says any failed clause refuses the candidate and forbids trying another cp
boundary on these data. That is the research result. D1320 landed concurrently with the run and
correctly states that a research mechanism refusal cannot silently overrule D1271's standing owner
ruling to fund a non-Maia base. The reserved third population remains unread; the product
disposition is routed to the RFC/owner rather than manufactured here. `[V]`

## 2. What the guard genuinely fixes

The inclusive mask retains candidates at `lossCp <= 250`, uses the identical legal-candidate mask
for engine and combined, and never falls back. Every position retains at least one move because the
complete legal set contains a zero-loss candidate. `[V]`

| fold | median surviving legal moves | singleton positions | engine mass removed | combined mass removed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| validation | 24 | 2.6% | 1.98% | 3.95% |
| confirmation | 23 | 7.4% | 1.92% | 3.26% |

The guard does not generally collapse choice to an engine best move: most positions retain dozens
of candidates. It does remove all probability mass above the threshold by construction. That is a
declared product constraint, not evidence that the underlying model learned a safe human
distribution. `[V]`

Expected loss and top-choice gates pass on both folds. Guarded combined expected loss is **50.4 cp
vs 50.1** on validation and **44.7 vs 46.6** on confirmation; top-choice agreement differs by less
than one percentage point in both. The safety/value problem is not what refuses this composition.
Its conditional choice fit and population coverage are inconsistent. `[V]`

## 3. The human-play cost is not evenly distributed

Pooled survival clears 90% twice, but the confirmation low-rating cell does not clear the 85%
floor. The other confirmation bands admit **95.0%** and **96.1%**. By ply, confirmation survival
falls to **11/14 (78.6%)** at ply 48; that slice was descriptive rather than gated, but it gives a
specific follow-up hypothesis: a fixed centipawn guard may suppress the later and lower-rated
human errors that distinguish a human policy from an engine-curated one. `[V]`

That hypothesis is not a causal finding and does not license a new threshold. The cell sizes are
small, and these folds are development data. It explains why the composition cannot honestly be
described as an unconditional human model: excluded observed moves have probability zero, not a
small smoothed probability. `[V]`

## 4. Numerical audit

The engine fits converge in eight iterations on both splits. Combined consumes all 80 iterations
and ends at gradient infinity norms **0.007954** and **0.006513**, above the `1e-6` target. The
architecture gate already fails without using this as an extra veto. It independently confirms
that no coefficient digest should be promoted as a frozen production model. `[V]`

The guard's inclusive boundary and renormalization have an able-to-fail fixture, including an
empty-mask refusal. The measured arm passes with three active tests and the unrelated D1297 full
arm skipped. `[V]`
