# Player habits are measurable; natural player types are not

**Question:** platform-alignment R12 — which descriptive player metrics are reproducible,
inspectable and sufficiently separate from strength to support a playful profile?

**Status:** short-session mechanical arm answered `[V]`; longitudinal and cross-time-control
generalisation remain unmeasured.

## Verdict

A transparent **continuous habit profile** is a viable research candidate. A natural archetype or
grandmaster-personality match is not.

Twelve of sixteen literal metrics pass a persistent split-half gate on a frozen population of 36
accounts × 200 blitz games. Their minimum measured short-session samples range from 25 to 200
games. The retained vector re-identifies 35 of 36 accounts across disjoint halves while a shuffled
identity control identifies none, and it does not predict within-band rating (`R² = -0.296`; the
injected rating control gives `R² = 1.0`). Yet no k=4–12 clustering survives resampling: median
adjusted Rand index is 0.251–0.417 against the predeclared 0.70 gate. `[V]`
`planning/platform-alignment/player-style/results.json`;
`tools/r12-player-style-harness/`.

That distinction is the product result: “you repeatedly choose these openings, structures and
clock patterns” can be grounded and drilled into. “You are The Positional Scientist” would invent
a categorical boundary this population does not contain. Style may be individual without falling
into stable types.

## 1. Competitor and literature claims are inputs, not specifications

Chessiverse's current page describes two incompatible input contracts. Its FAQ calls the product a
3–5-minute preference quiz and says it measures stated preferences, while the guide on the same
page says it imports up to 100 games, computes 51 metrics/eight axes and maps them to 30+
archetypes. It states a 12-game floor and “ideally 50+,” but publishes neither formulas nor a
stability curve. `[V]` [Chessiverse personality page](https://chessiverse.com/chess-personality).

ChessBase reports the opposite practical warning: results based on 100–200 games can fluctuate
substantially and a more natural curve begins beyond 200; it also shows that changing the historical
window can materially change the report. This is vendor experience, not a reproduced formula, but
it makes sample size and time window first-class test variables. `[P]`
[ChessBase 18 Style Report follow-up](https://en.chessbase.com/post/self-assessment-vs-chessbase-18-style-report).

Behavioral-stylometry research establishes that chess decisions can carry strong individual
identity: McIlroy-Young et al. report 98% identification among thousands of candidates from 100
labeled games using a transformer. That is not evidence for interpretable axes or natural
archetypes; it is evidence that “individual signature” and “named type” are different questions,
and that a style vector has privacy consequences. `[P]`
[McIlroy-Young et al., *Detecting Individual Decision-Making Style*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01366).

## 2. Frozen population and source correction

The initial plan depended on Lichess's public per-user game export. On 2026-08-20 both the API route
and the download route generated by the current profile form returned HTTP 404 for every tested
account. No metric had been scored. The acquisition contract was replaced with a frozen prefix of
the official CC0 monthly database, whose documentation explicitly permits partial Zstandard
decompression. `[V]` [Lichess open database](https://database.lichess.org/);
`planning/platform-alignment/player-style/plan.md` §Population.

The first 256 MiB compressed prefix covered 830,168 games / ten hours and supplied zero accounts
with 200 eligible blitz games. Before scoring, the source was widened once to exactly 2 GiB
compressed (`sha256:8502a22a…e3e59`). It covers 6,599,736 complete games from July 1 00:00:32 to
July 3 11:19:31 UTC; 2,660,480 pass the rated-standard-blitz/length header filter and 2,573,111
also parse legally through the opening reference window. `[V]`
`planning/platform-alignment/player-style/results.json`.

There are 190 accounts with at least 200 eligible appearances; seven carry a BOT title. The frozen
selection takes 12 non-bot accounts in each of 1000–1399, 1400–1799 and 1800–2199 by descending
appearance count, then their first 200 valid games. All 65 reserve candidates had at least 200
fully legal records, so validity filtering did not change the rank selection. The measured cohort
contains 7,200 games and 261,892 learner decisions. `[V]` same result and harness.

This is deliberately a **high-activity, 59-hour blitz population**. It can test whether a metric is
reproducible across disjoint games in one concentrated session. It cannot validate a profile across
weeks, rapid/classical play, repertoire changes or ordinary learner cadence. The measured floors
below are prototype floors for this population, not 1.0 defaults.

## 3. Metrics and denominators

Every choice metric is literal. Opening surprisal is same-rating-band move frequency at the exact
position through ply eight, admitted only at `N >= 50`. ECO entropy counts observed three-character
families. Fianchetto setup means the bishop and advanced b/g pawn occupy declared squares; the
screen requires the same-side knight on c3/f3/c6/f6; unblock means a learner move from that state
moves the knight and enlarges the bishop's geometric ray. Castling and clock arithmetic are exact.
`[V]` `planning/platform-alignment/player-style/plan.md` §Candidate metric registry;
`tools/r12-player-style-harness/measure.test.ts`.

Pawn, centre-pawn, early-queen, forcing, non-pawn-capture and reply-breadth metrics use an
opportunity denominator. At each eligible decision the harness subtracts the share of all legal
alternatives with the same event from the played event. This prevents “often pushes pawns” from
meaning merely “reaches positions with many pawn moves.” All promotion roles are enumerated and
synthetic controls cover promotion, capture/check, castling, color, clock and the fianchetto
screen/unblock. `[V]` same plan and harness.

The `/private/tmp` measurement artifact retains source game URL, ply, SAN/UCI and every per-decision
contribution for all 261,892 decisions. Committed results contain pseudonyms and aggregates only.
Thus each candidate number is drillable without publishing player histories. `[V]` harness output
assertion and `results.json` population counts.

## 5. The owner's fianchetto example separates configuration from consequence

The literal fianchetto setup is a strong short-session signature: it passes from 25 games. The
knight-screen configuration needs all 200 games. Across the cohort, 1,114/7,200 games reach a
fianchetto setup and 686 reach the named knight screen. `[V]` harness descriptives.

The subsequent choice is not stable. Learners made 4,473 decisions from a screened position and
moved the screen to expand the bishop ray 586 times (13.1%). Only 17 accounts have the event defined
for both colors; split-half rho is 0.188 and same-side agreement 50%. This population supports
“repeatedly builds this configuration,” not “likes discovered attacks” or “habitually uncovers the
bishop.” `[V]` same results.

The first run incorrectly reported 586/586 because its denominator admitted only knight moves. The
all-ones result was treated as an instrument alarm, discarded, and repaired before publication.
That is D603 and a reusable negative control: a conditional behavior rate must include decisions
where the event could have been declined. `[V]` plan correction and harness history.

## 6. Individual signature passes; archetypes fail

Using only the 12 persistently retained metrics, the rating-band-residualized half-A vector selects
the same account's half-B vector for 35/36 accounts (97.2%). Rotating identity labels yields 0/36.
This aligns in direction with published behavioral-stylometry evidence while using far simpler,
inspectable atoms. It does not validate the paper's model or population. `[V]` local result;
`[P]` [McIlroy-Young et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.01366).

Five-fold ridge regression from the vector to within-band rating produces `R² = -0.296`, worse than
predicting the fold mean; an injected rating-residual feature produces `R² = 1.0`. The separation
gate passes on this cohort. This is not proof that every future metric version is rating-free: the
test must rerun whenever the registry or reference population changes. `[V]` results.

Clustering fails decisively. For k=4–12, median account+game-bootstrap ARI ranges from 0.251 to
0.417, and seven of nine baseline solutions contain a one-account cluster. No value approaches the
0.70 stability gate. A nearest-grandmaster match would add an unmeasured, differently sampled
reference corpus and is therefore even less justified. `[V]` results.

## 7. Product consequences

The R12 evidence permits only a candidate **habit-card registry**:

- each row names a literal metric version, exact denominator, reference population/window, minimum
  sample, game/decision count, bootstrap interval and drill-down events;
- opening, structure, clock and choice rows remain continuous and separately hide/abstain;
- style bytes stay separate from rating, review verdicts and advice;
- a fun share card may render measured rows, but no categorical type or grandmaster identity ships
  from this experiment;
- the vector is behavioral identifying data and must feed R18's export/delete/share-by-consent
  audit before production.

The following remain refused for 1.0 on present evidence:

- Chessiverse-style 30+ archetypes or a “GM twin”;
- one global 12/50/100-game confidence label;
- “tactical,” “positional,” “aggressive,” “creative,” “patient” or similar compositions without a
  separately validated formula;
- interpreting failure to reach an endgame as a preference to avoid it;
- using the four refused metrics merely because their rank correlation looks high;
- LLM-written weaknesses or training advice from these atoms. R13 must define grounded coaching
  aggregation; law 8 still governs the prose.

## 8. Limits and residual experiment

1. The selection is deliberately biased toward unusually active blitz accounts. Ordinary learners
   may need more calendar time and may have different stability.
2. All 200 games sit within 59 hours. Session consistency may inflate re-identification and does not
   establish longitudinal stability.
3. Only Lichess blitz and three broad rating bands were measured; rapid, classical, Chess.com and
   imported OTB games remain untested.
4. Opening commonness uses the decision player's game rating, not an account-long rating estimate.
5. Exact-position counts through ply eight are descriptive population frequencies, not theory or
   move quality.
6. Bootstrap intervals quantify game sampling inside this fixed cohort, not population selection
   uncertainty.

The next validity arm should freeze accounts with games spread over at least eight weeks, compare
non-overlapping early/late windows and test blitz↔rapid transfer. Until that passes, the measured
floors authorize a prototype/decision, not production defaults.
