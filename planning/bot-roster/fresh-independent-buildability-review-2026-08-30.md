# Bot roster — fresh independent buildability and 1.0-depth review

- **Reviewed:** 2026-08-30
- **Input:** `rfc/bot-roster.md` after the D1601–D1609 author amendment
- **Verdict:** **RETURN TO AUTHOR / NOT ACCEPTED**
- **Reproduction:** `make bot-roster-fresh-review` — 5/5 blocker arms
- **Production status:** `BOT_POLICY_PROFILES` remains correctly empty

The measured four-band foundation, sealed guard dependency, legal-board pawn trait, grounded card
direction and atomic-route requirement survive. The review found five seams between those pieces
and the claimed full 1.0 roster. Two are internal contradictions, one makes the calibration plan
numerically false, and two allow “twelve personas” to ship with only three actual behaviors.

## B1 — the roster depends on a returned policy contract ([[D2233]])

`bot-policy.md` is returned on D2219–D2226: sampler/model authority, pawn classification, provider
delivery/health, route result, shared catalog, decision grammar and retry identity are open. The
roster consumes every one of those seams. Its header still describes a merely “repaired” dependency
and its acceptance criteria assume the atomic operation is available.

**Required repair:** keep the roster returned until the policy's next author repair survives fresh
review. Then refresh every profile declaration/card/receipt against the repaired policy types rather
than copying today's draft shapes.

## B2 — display bytes invalidate behavior calibration ([[D2234]])

Section 2 correctly declares display identity independent of policy and excludes it from behavior
and family equality. Sections 6–7 then hash the whole composition, including persona identity, and
say changing any name/avatar invalidates the calibration. A cosmetic asset or localized identity
therefore destroys a measurement of unchanged chess behavior. This is why D1610 unnecessarily
blocks the expensive ladder.

**Required repair:** publish separate policy/behavior and presentation digests. Calibration keys the
policy digest plus exact runtime/provider inputs; a full profile digest may bind the assembled card
and assets without laundering display into chess behavior. Cross an avatar-only change and a
policy-layer change in opposite directions.

## B3 — the calibration plan's declared size is arithmetically false ([[D2235]])

The table lists C1, C2, N, four A arms, four B arms, four P arms, G1 and G2: **17 arms**, not 16.
At the stated game counts the total is **13,200**, not 12,400. Resource/time estimates and the
completion receipt are therefore based on a population different from the normative table.

**Required repair:** derive arms and total games from one literal experiment manifest. The runner,
preregistration, progress output and receipt consume that manifest; hand-written totals become
tripwires only.

## B4 — “distribution acceptance” has no executable acceptance bounds ([[D2236]])

The supposed predeclared gate says histogram “shape agreement, bounds fixed before reading,” human
parameter “band envelope,” blunder-tail “shape,” and move match “band-peaked.” It provides no
distance statistic, binning, tolerance, lower-band envelope, multiple-comparison rule or pass/fail
algebra. It explicitly admits that the 1000/1400 Regan envelopes do not exist and that D1184 still
needs a new statistic/population. Exact-digest runs can therefore finish without any deterministic
way to decide whether a profile calibrated.

**Required repair:** research and preregister the replacement first. Publish a literal experiment
and gate manifest with population, controls, statistics, bins, uncertainty, thresholds, abstention
and multiplicity. A result receipt must be mechanically classifiable without prose judgement.

## B5 — twelve display identities collapse to three behaviors ([[D2237]])

The exact 4×3 product creates twelve persistent personas, but the four members of each family share
the same behavior transform and differ only in Maia band. Section 5 proposes eight more traits, yet
none enters `ROSTER`, none has a profile-construction rule, and D3 screens only four. Stage-B
candidate evidence is explicitly carried in records but forbidden from composition unless a later
owner decision funds it. Thus the “one registry, two consumers” foundation still cannot improve bot
decisions, and distinct names risk presenting identity breadth where only three behavior families
exist.

**Required repair:** distinguish the honest Stage-A 4×3 launch roster from the full personality
roadmap. Every proposed trait gets a closed disposition: refused, research-screened, measured, or
bound to named profiles. Add a researched evidence-to-trait adapter contract before any Stage-B
primitive affects moves; it must use registered value authority, the same safety/calibration gates
and no learner-derived covert difficulty. If the owner retains exactly three 1.0 families, cards and
positioning must say so rather than calling twelve display identities twelve personalities.
