# Assistance register — sixth fresh independent buildability review

- **Date:** 2026-08-31
- **Subject:** fifth-return author repair in `rfc/assistance-config-register.md`
- **Verdict:** returned on [[D2450]]–[[D2454]]
- **Production/protected design:** untouched

## What survives

The repair truthfully models workflow-v1's open/context-dependent parser, gives the future
exchange a named absence root, refuses landed-to-absent regression and broadens the proposed
permission claim beyond its union. Those are necessary corrections. They are not yet a complete
register contract because the adjacent resources still lack closed before/after authority and the
two active process RFCs prescribe mutually exclusive register architectures.

## D2450 — workflow-v2's exact delta is incomplete

The v1 authority population includes `WORKFLOW_CONTEXT_POLICIES` and `workflowContextPolicy`.
`intent-presets` adds `configClamp` to the former, so the whole-body semantic digest defined by
this RFC necessarily changes that node. Neither symbol appears in the claimed eight-symbol v2
transition. The v2 durable grammar also reaches receipt/override and field/module registry types
that are absent from the list.

Derive the transition from a complete before/after resolved type-and-operation graph. If the
author intends a narrower semantic slice, define and falsify that slice explicitly; a hand-picked
top-level symbol list cannot be called graph-derived.

## D2451 — permission composition is outside permission identity

The contract says every operation that returns, clamps or compiles `AssistancePermission` moves
the resource. The proposed implementation introduces `ConfigClamp` and composes it with
`pointwiseMin(accessPermission(...), contextClamp(...))`, but the exact five-symbol claim names
neither the clamp type nor the minimum operation. Changing the domain order or composition can
change every effective field while the registered identity stays still.

Close the resolved type/call graph and derive the claim from it. A fixture must mutate the order
and one minimum arm while leaving the union unchanged.

## D2452 — exchange has no landed semantic tree

`NumericResourceTreeState` records only head, history and an opaque digest. The RFC specifies how
absence is detected and lists ten symbols for first landing, but never defines the head-1 graph,
transitive type closure, canonical bytes or digest image. After v1 lands, a nested wire member or
compiler body can change at fixed head with no specified derivation that detects it.

Define one landed exchange tree and use it for the first claim, fixed-head drift and every later
transition. The first-delta list must be derived from that tree rather than substituted for it.

## D2453 — permission lifecycle cannot represent same-membership semantic changes

The permission resource includes operation semantics in its digest, while its only claim grammar
adds vocabulary members. A legitimate repair to `permittedAssistance`, `contextClamp` or
authoritative compilation with the same member set has no lane it can claim. The checker must
either reject that repair forever or permit fixed-identity drift.

Version the semantic-operation resource, or restrict the closed-vocabulary identity to the union
and register the operation graph separately. Cross a same-membership behavior change in both the
refused and lawful successor forms.

## D2454 — process integration is unordered and contradictory

This RFC adds literals to `RESOURCE_NAMES` and implements bespoke C9 branches. The active
`shared-resource-register-bootstrap` RFC deletes `RESOURCE_NAMES`, derives resources from a
catalogue and forbids resource-name-specific branching. Both edit `register-check` and README;
neither depends on or adapts to the other.

Choose one implementation order and one final architecture. The preferred repair is to express
assistance resources in the generic catalogue after that process contract is accepted, extending
its kind algebra only where absent or semantic-operation lifecycle proves it necessary.
